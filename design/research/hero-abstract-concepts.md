# Hero Abstract Concept Research

## Brief

A full-bleed 16:9 hero for an open delegation-rule registry. The image should feel dark, iconic, inspectable, and alive without becoming a generic crypto network, corporate particle wave, glassmorphism study, or cluttered technical diagram.

## Reference observations

### Capitolis motion rebrand

Source: https://www.itmatters.studio/work/capitolis-rebrand

- Composition: one controlled particle flow crossing large matte surfaces.
- Palette: black, graphite, soft grey.
- Finish: smooth solid forms contrasted with granular particles.
- Useful lesson: particles become legible when the surrounding surfaces stay calm.
- Avoid copying: its flowing-finance metaphor and exact surface forms.

### Maxim Zhestkov particle work

Source: https://fitc.ca/article/artist-profile-maxim-zhestkov/

- Composition: particle density creates volume and motion.
- Palette: highly controlled monochrome.
- Finish: cinematic simulation with architectural discipline.
- Useful lesson: movement can describe an invisible field.
- Risk: a full-frame simulation quickly becomes visual noise.

### Crou motion identity

Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/93364505/Crou-Water-brand

- Composition: a recognizable mark forms, fragments, and reforms through particles.
- Palette: black and white.
- Finish: dimensional particle motion tied directly to brand identity.
- Useful lesson: particles should behave according to the mark, not sit behind it.
- Risk: explosive fragmentation would communicate instability instead of a dependable registry.

### Milkinside dotted logo

Source: https://dribbble.com/shots/2904026-Milkinside-logo

- Composition: one centered geometric mark on black.
- Palette: pure monochrome and graphite.
- Finish: dot density creates depth while the silhouette remains readable.
- Useful lesson: a small particle vocabulary can still feel dimensional.
- Risk: building the whole Caveat logo from dots would weaken its sharp folded planes.

### P3P510 motion study

Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/55651635/P3P510-A-Case-Study-in-Motion-Design

- Composition: one solid logo object remains the constant while procedural systems act on it.
- Palette and finish: material and lighting vary, identity remains fixed.
- Useful lesson: the Caveat mark can become a repeatable motion container rather than one static illustration.
- Risk: showing many effects at once would turn the hero into a technology demo.

### 3D glass logo explorations

Source: https://dribbble.com/search/3d-glass-logo

- Composition: isolated volumetric marks with large negative space.
- Finish: refraction and internal depth make logos feel like physical objects.
- Useful lesson: transparency can communicate visibility and inspection.
- Risk: a fully glass logo would look trend-led, generic, and too close to glassmorphism.

## Synthesized visual language

The hero should treat the Caveat mark as one monumental physical instrument for seeing delegation data. Its outside planes remain matte, sharp, and nearly monochrome. Only the inner negative-space channel reveals motion. A small number of particles travel inside that channel, becoming visible because the registry exposes them. The particles never spread across the page. The background remains uninterrupted black, with enough negative space for centered typography and two actions.

### Core attributes

- One centered brand object.
- Recognizable Caveat silhouette at first glance.
- Matte white or pale-grey exterior planes.
- Smoked transparent or cutaway inner channel.
- Particles clipped strictly inside the channel.
- Twelve to thirty visible particles, not hundreds.
- Slow directional travel, not a vortex or explosion.
- One orange particle or trace as the active contribution.
- White and grey particles as indexed records.
- No lilac or mint in the hero unless testing proves one tiny verification signal is necessary.
- Black background with no decorative grid, circuit paths, or constellations.
- Centered or bottom-centered typography remains outside the busiest part of the mark.

## Concept directions

### 1. Registry Within

The Caveat logo is a thick 2.5D matte-white object floating on black. Its open internal gap is capped with dark transparent material. A restrained stream of record particles moves inside the gap as if the mark contains a visible registry.

Meaning: the product makes the records inside a permission system inspectable.

Strength: closest to the original user idea while preserving a premium logo silhouette.

Risk: full transparency could become glassmorphism. Keep the exterior matte and transparency limited to the channel.

### 2. X-Ray Mark

The logo appears as one solid white sculptural object. The surrounding image is completely still. Only the logo's negative-space opening behaves like an X-ray window, revealing particles moving behind the otherwise opaque object.

Meaning: the registry lets people see what is normally hidden inside delegation rules.

Strength: strongest metaphor and quietest composition.

Risk: the reveal must align precisely with the logo geometry or it will look like a masking trick.

### 3. Open Ledger

The two logo planes act like two floating archive shelves. Small white record objects enter from one side, align inside the gap, and remain individually visible. One orange record arrives last and expands the collection.

Meaning: an open registry that grows through contributions.

Strength: clearly communicates listing and extensibility.

Risk: can become too literal if particles resemble folders, cards, or database rows.

### 4. Permission Field

The logo is rendered as a thin white wireframe volume. Sparse particles move freely outside it, but become ordered into a straight readable sequence when passing through its opening.

Meaning: raw delegation information becomes structured through a shared standard.

Strength: explains transformation without additional interface copy.

Risk: wireframes and free particles may repeat the visual busyness of the rejected circuit hero.

### 5. Living Index

The logo begins as a solid white mark. On pointer movement, a small section becomes translucent and reveals a quiet internal particle layer. When the pointer leaves, it returns to solid white.

Meaning: evidence is available when the user chooses to inspect it.

Strength: gives the hero one memorable interactive moment without constant motion.

Risk: the static first frame must still be compelling and the interaction needs a clear text-safe area.

## Recommended direction

Combine `Registry Within` and `X-Ray Mark`.

Use a matte-white 2.5D Caveat mark with sharp folded planes. Keep the exterior solid and premium. Turn only the black negative-space gap into a smoked transparent viewing channel. Inside that channel, approximately eighteen soft white record particles travel slowly in one direction. One orange particle enters, joins the flow, and remains visible as a newly contributed record. The rest of the hero stays completely black and motionless.

This combination communicates three product truths with one visual behavior:

1. The mark is the registry boundary.
2. The inner records are visible and inspectable.
3. The registry remains open to new contributions.

## Generation guardrails

- Generate the isolated brand object and its internal behavior, not a complete website screenshot.
- Preserve the exact selected logo proportions.
- No text inside the generated image.
- No full-frame particles.
- No particle explosions or dissolves.
- No glossy rainbow refraction.
- No transparent glass exterior.
- No dramatic camera tilt.
- No glowing edges.
- No crypto coins, chains, locks, shields, cubes, or databases.
- Approve a static desktop frame before defining animation or mobile cropping.
